\section{Gradient-surgery baselines (single-seed; reported for completeness)}
\label{app:surgery}

We ran conflict-targeted gradient-surgery methods on the shared-MTP variant to
test directly whether removing directional conflict recovers the baseline.
\textbf{We do not rest any claim in the main text on these runs}, for three
reasons stated up front: (i) each is a \textbf{single seed} (seed 42); (ii) the
PCGrad baseline operates on \textbf{all parameters}, whereas per-row Gram--Schmidt
and Scatter operate on the \textbf{output head only}, so they are not strictly
comparable; and (iii) surgery uses \textbf{two backward passes} under bf16 (versus
the fused single pass of the main runs), introducing a ${\sim}0.02$--$0.04$ nat
numerical drift comparable to the smaller surgery effects. With those caveats, the
pattern is uniform: no surgery method recovers the CE-only baseline
($\text{A} = 3.985$), and none improves meaningfully on the matched shared-MTP
seed ($\text{B@42} = 4.390$).

This is what a norm-weighting/capacity account predicts, and it is stronger than
``nothing to project away.'' The opposed rows carry the majority of the
gradient-magnitude mass ($60$--$69\%$; Section~\ref{sec:emergent}), and a
projection acts on that mass-weighted vector, so there was substantial opposition
to remove; per-row Gram--Schmidt on the head targets exactly those rows and still
leaves the loss at the shared-MTP baseline. The measured opposition is
mass-dominant yet causally inert. A matched-seed,
single-pass replication at $n{=}3$ (identified in Section~\ref{sec:discussion})
would remove the drift caveat and is the right way to upgrade this from a
consistent-with observation to evidence. The AdamW negatives from the same phase
(A $=3.743/3.754$, B $=4.149/4.153$, $\text{G}_\text{nce}=3.778/3.781$ across seeds
42/123) reproduce the ordering under a second optimizer and are included in
\texttt{results/phase\_c/}.
