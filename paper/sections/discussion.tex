\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the measurements establish.} In this regime (a single shared tied output projection, ${\leq}350$M, undertrained) the standard cosine
conflict diagnostic misreads the CE/MTP interaction. The per-row gradients are
aligned (median $+1$), the aggregate is $\approx 0$, and the two decouple during
training in a pattern that points to support divergence rather than cancellation.
The next-token degradation is large and unambiguous, yet it coincides with
aligned gradients, so conflict-based remedies have essentially nothing to act on,
consistent with the surgery baselines in Appendix~\ref{app:surgery}, which
recover nothing. The lever that helps is capacity separation, though in our runs
it reduces rather than eliminates the gap.

\paragraph{What they do not establish.} We do not show that MTP \emph{as
practiced} (with separate per-horizon heads) degrades next-token modeling;
our formulation is a single tied head, and separate-head designs give the
auxiliary its own capacity precisely so that support cannot compete. We do not
establish causation between support divergence and the loss increase; we show
they co-occur and that interventions behave as a support/capacity account
predicts. And the effect lives in an undertrained, small-batch, ${\leq}350$M
regime where MTP is already documented to underperform \citep{zuhri2025top}.

\paragraph{Future work.} Four experiments would broaden the scope from ``the
diagnostic misreads this regime'' toward a causal, general statement: (1) a
\textbf{separate-head MTP} variant, to localize whether the degradation is
intrinsic to head-sharing; (2) \textbf{$n{=}3$ at 350M} plus a 124M CE-only
per-row baseline, to turn the single-seed scale observation into a measured
trend; (3) \textbf{matched-seed, single-pass gradient surgery} at $n{=}3$, to
remove the numerical-drift caveat; and (4) a \textbf{direct per-row-norm
measurement}, to upgrade support divergence from an inference (forced by aligned
per-row $+$ zero aggregate) to a measurement. For (4) we have already shown, via
an exact identity, that the aggregate collapse is driven by per-row norm
structure rather than by the typical per-row direction (Section~\ref{sec:emergent},
Figure~\ref{fig:normdecomp}), and we ship the instrument
(\texttt{measure\_norm\_support.py}) that logs the per-row norms and reports the
two statistics (norm-profile overlap, opposed-norm fraction) needed to separate
support divergence from a high-norm opposed minority; it runs on the existing
short diagnostic pass, so (4) is a single measurement rather than a new training
campaign.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Cosine similarity between task gradients is the default instrument for detecting
interference in multi-task and auxiliary-loss training. We showed that at a
language model's shared, tied output projection this instrument is misleading:
the per-row CE and MTP gradients are near-perfectly aligned in direction, the
aggregate cosine is driven to zero by disjoint support rather than by
cancellation, and the two metrics decouple during training. Adding the shared
auxiliary degrades next-token loss substantially, but not through the directional
conflict a cosine diagnostic would report. The operative axis is gradient
support, not direction, a distinction that explains why conflict-targeted
surgery does not help here and why capacity separation does. We deliver the result
on a fully reproducible testbed with a committed statistics script, and we are
explicit about the shared-head, small-scale scope and the experiments that would
extend it.
